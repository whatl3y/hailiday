# Experience specification

Hailiday shows a person their real home decorated for a holiday and helps them find the decorations. This document specifies what that person sees and does: every screen and state, the wording, the controls, and the rules that keep the product simple, honest and accessible. Engineers and coding agents implement `apps/web` from it; mechanics that belong to other specifications are linked, not restated.

**Owns:** the design system (tokens, picture surfaces, header tones, motion); screens and states; friction budget; copy deck; controls; allowance UX; sharing UX and share page content; report and feedback UX; accessibility requirements; mobile layout.
**Baseline:** planning baseline 2026-11-09; external facts retrieved 2026-11-08.

## Experience principles

The product thesis is Fixed ([D-49](../decisions.md#d-49); [PLAN §1](../PLAN.md#1-product-promise)): the experience must be simple, beautiful and emotional, because those qualities, not the AI capability, are why people choose Hailiday, and the feeling of seeing their own place transformed is what nudges them to decorate it themselves. The landing and interaction model is Fixed ([D-06](../decisions.md#d-06)): a seasonal hero for the upcoming holiday, one prominent address input that accepts any US address, a quieter upload route, easy address and holiday switching, and nothing else competing for attention. The result must be a faithfully decorated real photograph ([D-07](../decisions.md#d-07)). The principles below turn those decisions into rules every screen follows. When a screen must choose between the thesis and another feature, control or notice, the thesis wins; safety, honesty and legal notices are never dropped, and the [friction budget](#friction-budget) places each one.

| Principle | Rule | What it forbids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The reveal is the moment | The first time a visitor sees their own place decorated is the product's central moment. A look that arrives while the person watches arrives as a reveal: the picture shows the earlier picture, then a divider sweeps once across it and uncovers the new look ([reveal](#reveal)). The picture is the first viewport, and everything else sits under it (the action row and collapsed rows), so nothing but the required labels and attribution shares the moment. | Pop-ups, prompts, sign-up walls or the shop panel above the result at the reveal; notices covering the image. |
| Warm, celebratory voice | Copy is short, warm and specific to the holiday and the place, written like a friend who loves the holidays (for example "Your porch, ready for Halloween"); the [copy deck](#copy-deck) follows this voice. | Technical jargon, sales language, urgency, guilt or scarcity. |
| Nudge, never pressure | Shop the look follows the reveal and invites the visitor to recreate the look, with honest relationship labels and the ad disclosure ([commerce spec](commerce.md#shop-the-look)). A visitor who only wants to enjoy the picture loses nothing. | Countdown timers, fake scarcity, auto-opening shop panels, purchase prompts before the reveal, repeated nagging. |
| Quiet UI | Everything that is not a picture is a minimal black-and-white system: a white page, near-black ink and hairlines, with photographs as the only color apart from one small holiday-colored dot beside a holiday's name and the string of lights that hangs over a picture while its look is being made ([Light string and status card](#light-string-and-status-card)). Wherever there is a picture, the picture is the page ([design system](#design-system)). One wordmark, one hero, one input. Legal and informational links live in the footer. Options stay out of sight until the person wants them: on the result page they sit in collapsed rows ([result page](#below-the-card)). | Dashboards, dense forms, banners, modals that are not triggered by the person's own action or content. |
| Photo-first | The photograph is the largest thing on every screen and reaches both edges of the viewport: the landing opens on one full-screen photograph with the headline and the address dock resting on it, and a picture of the person's place fills the first viewport on an ink stage. Text and controls sit on paper under the picture, and nothing covers attribution or the AI preview label. Every image is a real photograph or hyperrealistic and indistinguishable from one ([D-49](../decisions.md#d-49)). | Boxes, borders, rounded frames or card shadows around a picture; heavy overlays, decorative chrome (apart from the light string), controls layered over a person's picture (apart from the Before / AI preview switch and, while a look is being made, the status card with its Cancel); illustrations, cartoons, clip art or animation standing in for imagery. |
| Honest states | Every state names what is true: which photo is shown, where it came from, whether it is being decorated, whether a person is reviewing it, what is left of the allowance. A synthesized house, a neighbor's building or a gray placeholder is never shown as the person's place. | Placeholder imagery standing in for the address, silent failures, controls that appear to work but do nothing. |
| One primary action per screen | Each screen has exactly one primary button; everything else is secondary or a link. | Two equal calls to action; wizards. |
| No fake progress | Waiting screens show what the render job reports: the label of its current stage, a trail of the stages it has reported so far (each once, the current one marked) and, when relevant, the queue position. The notes shown during a wait are statements and tips, never steps. | Percentages, timed progress bars, invented sub-steps, a step shown before the job has reported it. |
| Nothing fails silently | At a limit the controls stay enabled and explain what happened and when it resets. While a look is being made, the controls that would start another look or change the place are locked, visibly, and say when they come back. Every error code in [data-and-api.md#error-codes](data-and-api.md#error-codes) has a copy key in this document. | Disabled controls without explanation, generic "error" toasts. |

## Design system

The design has two kinds of surface. **Paper** is the white page: ink type, hairlines, Outfit for text and JetBrains Mono for small technical text, solid ink pill buttons, uppercase micro-labels, and one small holiday-colored dot, the first of the two places the interface carries color. The second is the string of lights that hangs over a picture while its look is being made ([Light string and status card](#light-string-and-status-card)). A **picture surface** is a photograph or a picture stage that reaches the viewport's left and right edges, with white type and minimal controls resting on it. Colors come from `--paper`, the inks and the stage tokens below, and nowhere else. A picture has no border, rounded frame, card shadow or side gutter on a phone; the card preview inside the Share sheet is the one framed picture, and it belongs to a sheet.

### Tokens

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--stage` | `#0d0d0f` | The ground behind a picture, the ink header band, and the browser's theme color on picture screens |
| `--on-stage` | `#ffffff` | Type and marks on a picture surface |
| `--on-stage-muted` | `rgb(255 255 255 / 0.72)` | Secondary type on a picture surface (the header's other links, a counter) |
| `--stage-line` | `rgb(255 255 255 / 0.14)` | Hairlines on ink (the holiday chip's outline, a hover fill) |
| `--scrim-rgb` | `13 13 15` | The only color a gradient or scrim is mixed from, as `rgb(var(--scrim-rgb) / a)` |
| `--glass-dark` | `rgb(17 17 19 / 0.66)` | Marks and pills on a picture (the AI preview mark, the eyebrow pill, the holiday chip on a photograph) |
| `--header-height` | 72 px, 56 px under 600 px | The header's height on every page; a stage reserves it above itself |
| `--stage-peek` | 104 px, 88 px under 600 px | What stays visible under a stage in the first viewport |
| `--dock-radius`, `--dock-shadow` | 20 px; `0 24px 64px -24px rgb(13 13 15 / 0.5)` | The white dock that rests on a photograph |
| `--dur-drift` | 24 s | The hero photograph's one slow drift |
| `--dur-reveal` | 1000 ms | The reveal's sweep |
| `--bulb-1`, `--bulb-2`, `--bulb-3` | Three colors per holiday (table in [Light string and status card](#light-string-and-status-card)); warm white when no holiday is chosen | The bulbs of the light string, and nothing else |

The stage's 20 px matte (`--stage-matte`, from 600 px wide) is set in the stage's own stylesheet, not in the token file. Where the ink ground shows beside a picture it is a quiet vignette, `radial-gradient(120% 90% at 50% 40%, #1b1b1e 0%, var(--stage) 70%)`, not a flat slab.

### Size and cropping

- A picture is shown as large as the viewport allows and never above its natural pixel size. The one exception is a Street View frame laid out at its look's size ([below](#street-view-frame-at-its-looks-size)).
- A person's own picture and every Street View frame are contained, never cropped. A raw Street View frame is never blurred or tinted, and nothing covers it but its marks and, while its look is being made, the light string along its top edge and the status card under it; neither blurs it or comes near its lower corners. Only the landing photograph and the other stock hero photographs cover their frame, around the crop's focal point. The Gallery and Your places crop their thumbnails by their own rules ([Gallery](#gallery), [Your places](#your-places)); the viewer shows the whole picture.
- Every picture's box is reserved from its size, so nothing shifts as pictures load.

### Hero frame

A hero frame is a full-viewport photograph with content resting on it. It is as tall as the first viewport (`100svh`, with a `100vh` fallback) and never under 560 px, reaches both edges, sits on the `--stage` ground and clips its overflow. The season's hero covers it around its crop's focal point: the portrait crop on any viewport taller than wide (media query `(orientation: portrait)`), the 3:2 crop otherwise. The photograph drifts once from scale 1 to 1.06 over `--dur-drift` and then rests. It stops one pixel short of the frame's lower edge, so the browser counts it as the page's largest paint; the drift's first frames cover that pixel. Gradient scrims, mixed only from `--scrim-rgb`, sit where white type rests and nowhere else, and as lightly as the contrast rules allow.

The hero frame is used by the landing, by the upload entry (`/upload`, and the upload step of a project until a photo is chosen), by the not-found page and by the No Street View screen. Its content block rests on the photograph; controls live in the **dock**, a white panel (radius `--dock-radius`, shadow `--dock-shadow`, ink type) that is paper again inside, focus ring included. A new photograph crossfades in over the one on screen once it has loaded (900 ms).

### Picture stage

A stage is the ground a picture is shown on. It is the full width of the viewport (it leaves any column without causing sideways scroll), ink with the vignette above, and holds the picture contained and centered in a box of the picture's own aspect ratio, at the largest size that fits and never above its natural size. It reads that size from `--stage-w` and `--stage-h`, which the app and the server-rendered share page both set, so the box exists before the picture loads.

- **From 600 px wide**, a stage that fills the viewport is as tall as the first viewport under the header less `--stage-peek` less the 20 px matte (the confirm screen of a view from the road raises the peek to 296 px, so its line, its views and the look buttons show without scrolling), never taller than the picture at the full width of the screen and never above its natural height. The floor is 320 px, or the room there is when that is less, so the peek stays visible on a short or landscape screen.
- **Under 600 px** the picture is full bleed, as tall as the picture at full width, up to the first viewport under the header less `--stage-peek`; a tall portrait, or a square on a small phone, is letterboxed on the ink ground. The project screens raise the peek to 144 px (220 px while the switch row sits under the picture, [Phones](#phones-the-picture-carries-nothing-but-its-marks)) and the share page to 184 px (248 px with a switch), so the caption, the title and the actions stay in the first viewport.
- **The matte.** From 600 px the stage keeps 20 px of ink under the picture, inside the stage, so a pale picture never melts into the paper below it. Under 600 px there is no matte.
- **The ink header.** Every screen that shows a stage wears the ink header band ([Header tones](#header-tones-and-theme-color)), so a pale picture never melts into a white header either.
- Another fill, `picture`, makes the stage exactly as tall as the picture at full width.

Screens with a stage: the result, waiting, a pending look, a render error that keeps its picture, confirm place, the public share page, and the upload step once a photo is chosen. The paper under a stage lines up with the picture's edges and is at least 55 rem wide on wide screens (54 rem on the share page).

### Ambient layer

From 900 px wide a stage repeats its rendering, or the person's uploaded photo, behind the picture: covering the stage, blurred (48 px), saturated (1.15), darkened (brightness 0.5) and scaled 1.15, darker still toward the edges so the picture is the brightest thing on the ground, and fading in over 1200 ms. It is decorative (`aria-hidden`), loads at low priority and never delays the picture. The gallery viewer's ground works the same way, from the rendering and never from the before photo.

The layer is absent under 900 px, under reduced motion it appears without the fade, and it is absent for a raw Street View frame: the frame on the confirm screen, and the Street View source on the waiting screen and under a pending look, are shown on the plain vignette.

### Light string and status card

While a look is being made, whether it is a place's first look or another look, two things make the wait plain on every screen size: a string of holiday lights along the top of the stage, and a status card that names the step the job is really in. They appear when the look starts and go when it arrives, and the [reveal](#reveal) follows. Nothing else of the screen moves, because both sit in space the screen already holds. What the card says and when is in [Waiting and progress](#waiting-and-progress); what is locked meanwhile is in [Changing holiday, style and lighting](#changing-holiday-style-and-lighting).

**The light string** hangs from the stage's top edge (directly under the ink header) across the stage's full width. It is a thin wire pinned at both edges and at every swag length, sagging between pins in even swags, with small bulbs hung from it. It is drawn (SVG, no image), decorative (`aria-hidden`, no tab stop, no function) and 36, 40 or 44 px deep at most. It never comes near a picture's lower corners. Its tier follows the viewport's width:

| Viewport width | Tier | Swags | Swag length | Sag | Bulbs per swag | Bulb spacing | Bulb radius | Depth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 320 | compact (under 600) | 3 | 106.7 | 14 | 3 | 35.6 | 3.5 | 36 |
| 390 | compact | 4 | 97.5 | 14 | 3 | 32.5 | 3.5 | 36 |
| 768 | medium (600 to 1023) | 6 | 128 | 18 | 4 | 32 | 4 | 40 |
| 1024 | wide (1024 and up) | 7 | 146.3 | 22 | 4 | 36.6 | 4.5 | 44 |
| 1440 | wide | 10 | 144 | 22 | 4 | 36 | 4.5 | 44 |
| 1920 | wide | 13 | 147.7 | 22 | 4 | 36.9 | 4.5 | 44 |

Lengths are CSS pixels. The swag length is the tier's target (104, 128 or 144 px) rounded to a whole number of swags across the stage. The wire is white at 62% over a 3 px under-wire of `rgb(var(--scrim-rgb) / 0.5)`, so it reads on a white ceiling and on ink. Each bulb has a socket, a body in its colour with a thin scrim edge (so a pale bulb stays visible on a pale picture), a soft glow of the same colour and a small white shine. The bulbs wear three colors per holiday, as tokens in the token file (the only new colors; components use `var(--bulb-n)`), taken from the look's holiday, not the page's:

| Scope | `--bulb-1` | `--bulb-2` | `--bulb-3` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No holiday chosen | `#ffd9a0` | `#fff4dc` | `#ffc46b` |
| `halloween` | `#ff8a2a` | `#a970ff` | `#ffe9c4` |
| `thanksgiving_us` | `#ffb347` | `#e8703a` | `#ffe2b0` |
| `christmas` | `#ff5a52` | `#3ccf7e` | `#ffd27a` |
| `new_years_eve` | `#ffc93c` | `#ffe9b0` | `#cfdcff` |

Each set starts from the holiday's dot, brightened to glow on ink, plus two companions. The colors are tokens so they can change without touching a component.

The bulbs twinkle in a wave: glow and body rise and fall over 2.4 s, each bulb 120 ms ahead of the one before it in the cycle (a negative animation delay of 120 ms per bulb), so the light travels along the string toward the first bulb, from right to left. The string arrives with a 480 ms fade and a 6 px drop and leaves at once when the look arrives (there is no exit animation). The bulbs answer the pointer: a pass or a tap on a bulb changes its colour to the next of the holiday's three (a 200 ms fade) and sends a ripple of flashes along the string, up to eight bulbs each way, each a little later and fainter. Nothing is counted, stored or sent, and a touch that starts on the string still scrolls the page. Under reduced motion the bulbs are lit and still, a poke changes a color and nothing more, and the string arrives at once.

**The status card** says which step the job is in. It holds, top to bottom: for another look, a small uppercase line naming the look being made (holiday, style and lighting, in the form of the caption's label); the stage label at title size with three pulsing dots after it; the trail of stages reported so far ([Waiting and progress](#waiting-and-progress)); and a foot line with the hint (`wait.usuallyAbout` "Usually a minute or two", the queue position or the review note) and Cancel. It sits in one of three places:

| Placement | Where | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On the stage | Hung from the string by two hangers at the top centre of the stage, on dark glass, 23 rem wide (never wider than the stage less 2 rem) | The screen is at least 600 px wide and at least 560 px tall, for an upload and for a raw Street View frame alike |
| On paper, first look | Where the title will be, directly under the caption, with the notes under it | A first look on any smaller screen, or a project with no picture yet |
| Band, another look | Over the title in the space the title holds (the stage label and, under it on one line, the last three trail entries) | Another look on any smaller screen |

On the stage the card's top edge is 44 px (medium) or 50 px (wide) below the stage's top. From 600 px wide it hangs 84 px down (`CARD_TOP_BELOW_SWITCH`) when the picture carries the AI preview mark at its upper left, that is for the look of a Street View place (the mark hangs below the string and the card must not cover it), and between 600 and 1023 px also when the Before / AI preview switch rests on the stage, so the card meets neither. Phones keep the card off the stage. The glass is `rgb(var(--scrim-rgb) / 0.78)` with a `--stage-line` hairline, a soft shadow and no backdrop blur (a raw Street View frame is never blurred). The card is about 116 px tall on a first look and 140 px with the look's line, one line more when the trail wraps. On a Street View frame the card's lower edge is at most 242 px from the stage's top (280 px while the lock line shows), so at least 84 px above the frame's lower edge stay clear of the card and Google's logo and copyright are never reached. Under 600 px Cancel does not sit in the card: for a first look it is alone in the row directly under the picture, where the result's switch will be, and for another look it is at the start of that row, with the switch at its end, so nothing moves when the look arrives.

The stage label is 20 px semibold white on the stage, in the result title's size on paper and in the band (a little smaller on phones, one line, cut with an ellipsis). The trail's words are 12 px medium; a stage passed is `--on-stage-muted` (`--ink-muted` on paper) with a check, the current one white (ink on paper) with a pulsing dot. The card arrives with a 480 ms fade after a 120 ms delay. The lock line `controls.locked` shows for 4 s when a locked control is used: as a pill under the stage card, in place of the hint on the paper card, and in place of the trail line in the band; no card changes size.

**Marks hang below the string.** While a look is being made, a mark at a picture's top edge hangs 36 to 44 px lower (the string's depth), over 280 ms: the AI preview mark of a Street View look at the upper left, and the Fixture mark in development at the upper right where no switch is on the stage. The AI preview mark of an upload is at the lower right and does not move. Google's logo and copyright are never reached: the string ends 44 px from the top and the card is placed as above.

**What differs by origin.** A Street View frame has no ambient layer (the plain vignette, as before) and keeps its marks as above; an upload shows its ambient layer from 900 px, and a short, very wide photo can be covered more by the card and carries no attribution. The sheen is the same for both.

### Street View frame at its look's size

A Street View look is rendered on the 1024 by 1024 working grid. Wherever a raw frame stands in for the look that is coming (confirm place, waiting, a pending look with a Street View source), it is laid out as if its natural size were that of the newest look already made from that source, or 1024 by 1024 when there is none, and scaled with the browser's smooth filtering. So the stage, the picture box and the title line do not move when the look arrives, and a frame is not left between wide ink bars. Nothing else changes about a raw frame: it is whole, has no ambient layer, and keeps Google's logo (lower left) and copyright (lower right) visible and uncovered. The zoomed frame that replaces the wide one at the look that frames a place is a square of the same size, with the marks in the same corners, so the stage does not change when it fades in ([Waiting and progress](#waiting-and-progress)).

### Header tones and theme color

The header is `--header-height` tall on every page, in one of three tones:

| Tone | Where | Header |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `paper` | Gallery, Your places, account, legal and information pages, opt-out, sign-in pages, loading and error states without a picture, a revoked or expired share link | On the white page, ink type |
| `photo` | The landing, the upload entry, the not-found page, the No Street View screen and the upload step before a photo is chosen (every hero frame) | Laid over the top of the photograph, transparent, with the brand and links in white (`--on-photo`) and the holiday chip on `--glass-dark` (8 px backdrop blur); the frame's top scrim keeps them at 4.5:1. Class `site-header--photo`, page class `page--photo` |
| `stage` | Result, waiting, a pending look, a render error with a picture, confirm place, the active share page (server-rendered and in the app), the upload step once a photo is chosen | Its own band on the ink ground (`--stage`) directly above the stage: the brand and the current link in white, the other links at 72% white, the holiday chip outlined with a 1 px `--stage-line` pill, focus rings white with an ink edge. Class `site-header--stage`, page class `page--stage` |

The browser's `theme-color` follows the tone: `#0d0d0f` on photo and stage pages, `#ffffff` on paper pages. The app sets it on every navigation; the server sets it in the HTML it renders, for the landing and the share page (a revoked or expired share link is paper and white), and for the app's routes it serves (ink for `/p/:projectId`, `/upload` and the not-found fallback; white for `/gallery`, `/places`, `/account`, `/auth/verify`, the legal pages and the opt-out page), so a direct load shows the right browser bar before any script runs.

### Phones: the picture carries nothing but its marks

Under 600 px nothing but the marks (the AI preview mark, the Fixture mark in development), the comparison divider's handle and, while a look is being made, the light string along the picture's top edge lies on a picture. The Before / AI preview switch sits on paper in a row directly under the stage, right-aligned with 44 px targets; from 600 px it rests in the stage's upper right corner as a dark glass pill (white type on `rgb(var(--scrim-rgb) / 0.66)`, at least 4.5:1 even over a white picture) and never over the picture's lower corners. The row under the stage holds the switch alone: the look's label is not repeated there, because the title names the holiday and the "Try another look" row names the look. On the waiting screen the same row holds Cancel alone, where the switch will be, so nothing moves when the look arrives; while another look is being made it holds Cancel at its start and the switch at its end. On the share page the switch has the first row under the picture to itself and the look's line follows it.

### Motion

Motion is quiet and optional, and none of it moves layout. The hero photograph drifts once; a new hero crossfades over the old; the landing's type and dock rise in over the photograph; a look arrives by the [reveal](#reveal); a picture that is being decorated carries a slow soft sheen (fading out before its lower edge, where Google's marks sit), the light string twinkles in a wave along the stage's top edge and answers the pointer, the status card's label pulses and the notes change every 7 s; the upload step's stage carries a 2 px progress hairline along its lower edge; the ambient layer fades in; the share page's picture arrives with a 1.2 s fade; the gallery viewer fades in and out. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` all of it is off: the drift, the crossfade, the rises, the sheen, the reveal and the ambient fade; the light string is lit and still, the pulse is three still dots and the notes change only when asked; the upload step's progress hairline stays as a still line.

### Contrast, focus and targets

Text over a photograph meets the contrast rules in [Visual and motion](#visual-and-motion). Focus on a picture surface is a white ring with an ink edge. The status card is white type on `rgb(var(--scrim-rgb) / 0.78)`, so its secondary type (white at 72%) keeps 4.5:1 over a white picture. Every interactive target is at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, including the small Before / AI preview switch on the example cards (a 32 px pill drawn inside 44 px targets). On Street View-origin pictures Google's logo and copyright stay visible and uncovered at every size, nothing overlays those corners, the credit line prints once, and the AI preview mark keeps the corners set in [AI preview badge placement](#ai-preview-badge-placement).

## Friction budget

"Dead simple" is a budget: at most one unconditional blocking step before the first render on each path. Conditional interrupts are allowed only when the person's own address or photo triggers them, and each is one tap to resolve.

| Path | Unconditional blocking steps before the first render | Conditional interrupts (triggered by the input) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address, `matchClass` = `auto_start` | 0. The render starts as soon as discovery returns `auto_start`; the waiting screen carries a passive "Not your place?" escape. | None. |
| Address, `matchClass` = `confirm`, manual entry, or off-season | 1. "Is this the right place?" (off-season adds a holiday choice on the same screen). | None. |
| Address, source mode `display_only` (interim experience under `STREET_VIEW_USE_POLICY=display_only`, or for contributor imagery under `STREET_VIEW_CONTRIBUTOR_IMAGERY=display_only`, [D-16](../decisions.md#d-16)) | 1. The age-and-rights checkbox on the upload step that follows confirmation. | People detected; scene type below the confidence floor. |
| Upload | 1. The age-and-rights checkbox ([D-17](../decisions.md#d-17)). | People detected ([D-18](../decisions.md#d-18)); scene type below the confidence floor. |

The bot check at session creation ([D-38](../decisions.md#d-38)) is invisible unless the challenge fails, in which case `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` adds one exceptional step; it is not counted in the budget.

A top gradient band keeps the white type at 4.5:1. Under 420 CSS pixels the pill's padding is 8 px; under 380 only the label shows, with no chevron, and the holiday name never wraps, so the brand, the holiday name and Gallery stay on one line without sideways scrolling at 320 px. Under 600 px it sits at the top under the header, and the lower part of the screen is photograph alone.

### Scrims

The photograph stays a photograph: the landing's scrims are mixed only from `--scrim-rgb`, and neither the header band nor the headline's ellipse shows an edge. Both are eased: the alpha falls along an ease-out curve written as at least six color stops, reaching exactly 0, with no step larger than 0.08 alpha between neighbouring stops.

- **The header band** (every hero frame carries it): 0.62 alpha at the top of the frame, near 0.6 behind the header, easing out to exactly 0 at twice `--header-height` along `0.62·(1 − t³)²`, written as 15 stops. It keeps the header's white type at 4.5:1.
- **The headline, from 600 px:** one soft ellipse around its two lines, sized in the headline's own em so it scales with the type: a box 13.6em by 4.4em set 1.55em above and 3.3em to the left of the headline's corner, so the ellipse is centred 3.5em in from the first letter and 0.65em below the first line's top, with radii of 6.8em and 2.2em. It is a `radial-gradient(closest-side, …)` whose alpha follows `0.6·(1 − r^4.5)²`: it holds near 0.6 behind the words and settles to exactly 0 at the rim, written as 17 stops; its darkest point is 0.6. The soft ink shadow is set in em too: `0 1px 2px` at 0.4, `0 0 0.06em` at 0.45, `0 0 0.16em` at 0.45, `0 0.08em 0.3em` at 0.4 and `0 0.22em 0.6em` at 0.4. The scrim's alpha is under 0.05 over the right 30% of the screen, over everything more than 140 px above the eyebrow (outside the header band), and over the photograph beside and below the dock (the ellipse runs on behind the dock, which is opaque).
- **The headline, under 600 px:** a band of 0.4 alpha from screen edge to screen edge, from the eyebrow's top to the dock's top, with soft edges, and the shadow `0 1px 2px` at 0.3, `0 0 18px` at 0.42 and `0 0 48px` at 0.32. Its alpha is under 0.05 everywhere below the dock's top edge.
- Nothing else on the photograph carries a scrim: the eyebrow, the holiday chip and the continue pill are glass pills, and everything else is in the dock. The rest of the photograph is as shot.

Contrast is measured, not assumed ([Visual and motion](#visual-and-motion)).

### Before the script runs

The server-rendered shell paints the hero frame: the header laid over it (the brand and the holiday label of the deep-linked or default holiday, or "Choose a holiday" off-season, plus the Gallery link), the photograph (`<picture>` with `srcset`, `sizes` `100vw`, `fetchpriority` high, the crop's focal point as the frame's `--hero-x` and `--hero-y` and `--hero-portrait-x` and `--hero-portrait-y` from the hero content), the scrim layer, and the content block with every app block in the same order and with the same classes (the eyebrow pill, the headline, then the dock with the subhead, the address field with its placeholder `landing.addressPlaceholder`, the routes with a real `/upload` link, the age line and the Thanksgiving invitation when the holiday asks for it). Before and after the app starts the layout is identical, at every viewport size, for new and returning visitors. The server sends the ink `theme-color`. The head, from the web build's `index.html`, also carries the brand mark as the tab icon (`/favicon.svg`, served from the app origin) and a preload of the latin Outfit face, so the server-painted headline does not swap faces. When the app starts, it adopts the server's photograph in place (never fetched or painted twice) and replaces the rest with its own; its entrance continues the server's staggered rise from the same point (read from the progress of the server's animations) instead of playing again, the photograph's drift continues from the server's progress the same way, and both appear at once when the browser cannot report that progress ([../architecture.md#landing-and-share-page-delivery](../architecture.md#landing-and-share-page-delivery)).

### Holiday chip and picker

The picker lists the holidays returned by `GET /v1/experience` in the order the holidays spec defines, with the current one marked. In-window, the chip shows the default holiday; off-season it reads `landing.chooseHoliday` "Choose a holiday" and the picker is expanded by default. A deliberate choice persists for the session and the project, and an address change never resets it (rule in [holidays-and-content.md#windows-and-selection](holidays-and-content.md#windows-and-selection)). Deep links with `?holiday=` preselect the chip.

### Thanksgiving emphasis (Proposed, D-11)

During the Thanksgiving window the hero is the candlelit dining room and the address input stays primary ([D-06](../decisions.md#d-06)). Under the age line, in the dock, a one-line invitation `landing.thanksgivingInvite` appears: "Hosting this year? Upload a photo of your dining room or table to see it set for Thanksgiving." The same invitation appears as a dismissible band on Thanksgiving exterior results (`result.thanksgivingInvite`). No other holiday carries an invitation in the US Release. The emphasis is data-driven: the client shows it when the holiday's `journeyEmphasis` in `GET /v1/experience` asks for it ([data-and-api.md#http-api](data-and-api.md#http-api)).

### Off-season

The hero is the neutral seasonal scene, the chip reads "Choose a holiday", the picker lists all supported holidays, and no render auto-starts: an `auto_start` discovery lands on the confirm screen with the holiday choice required. Copy and behavior rules are in [holidays-and-content.md#off-season-behavior](holidays-and-content.md#off-season-behavior).

### Returning visitor and paused service

If the browser remembers the session's last project, `landing.continueBanner` "Continue with your last place" is a pill on the photograph (`--glass-dark`, 44 px tall, with an arrow) that links to it. From 600 px it sits first in the content block, above the eyebrow, because the block rests at the bottom of the frame; on phones it sits below the dock. In both places it moves nothing the server painted. While time-based deletion is on, the pill shows only when that project was opened within the last 24 hours (the anonymous retention window). Only the device knows its last project, so the server cannot paint it. When the experience snapshot reports a service pause, the budget-paused banner (`error.BUDGET_PAUSED`) sits first in the content block as a white pill; the field still works for lookups, and the pause is explained again at the render request.

## Address entry and confirmation

This section specifies the interface only. Suggestion mechanics, session tokens, resolution, precision, candidate viewpoints, match classes and spend limits are in [address-and-street-view.md](address-and-street-view.md) (sections [Address entry](address-and-street-view.md#address-entry), [Manual entry](address-and-street-view.md#manual-entry), [Match confidence and auto-start](address-and-street-view.md#match-confidence-and-auto-start), [Viewpoint adjustment](address-and-street-view.md#viewpoint-adjustment)).

### Autocomplete

- The field is an ARIA combobox with a listbox popup (pattern and keys in [Accessibility](#accessibility)). Minimum characters, debounce, stale-request cancellation and the no-re-query rule are client behaviors defined in the address spec; the client never sends the typed text anywhere but the suggestions endpoint.
- Suggestions show up to 5 rows, each with the main text (street) and secondary text (city, state), plus the provider attribution the address spec requires for suggestions shown off a map. Suggestions from the fixture address provider are not Google's and never carry the Google attribution. The list paints above everything that follows the field in the landing dock (routes, age line, invitation), however the person arrived at `/`; the panel's entrance animation leaves no layer behind once it has played.
- States and copy: typing below the minimum shows nothing; loading shows a subtle indicator inside the field (no spinner row) and leaves the last settled state (the open list or the no-results line) in place until the new answer arrives, so nothing under the field moves while a request runs and consecutive empty answers keep the same line; no results shows `address.noResults` (on `/dont-decorate-my-home`, which has neither manual entry nor photo upload, `optout.noResults`); a non-US resolution shows `error.ADDRESS_OUT_OF_SCOPE`; `RATE_LIMITED` on suggestions is retried silently after `Retry-After` and shows `error.RATE_LIMITED` only if it persists; a session that cannot start is shown at once with its own line and "Try again" ([Starting the session](#starting-the-session)); `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` renders the bot challenge inline under the field and retries the last action once on success.
- Sample mode: when the experience payload's `fixtureCapabilities` lists `address_lookup` (outside production only), the address lookup has no Google Maps keys and knows only the fixture sample places. No results then shows `address.noResultsSample` instead of `address.noResults` (on `/dont-decorate-my-home`, which has no photo upload, `optout.noResultsSample`), followed by a group (`address.samplePlaces`) of one-tap sample places (`14 Lantern Hill`, `418 Larkspur`), and the live region announces `address.noMatchesSample` instead of "No matches"; a tap fills the field, searches at once with focus kept in the field, and opens the list, and choosing a row resolves as usual. Nothing changes before the person types.
- Selecting a suggestion calls resolve and navigates to `/p/:projectId`. The discovery runs there; while it runs the screen shows `confirm.finding` "Looking for a street photo of this place…" with no image. A gray or sample image is never shown as the address's photo.

### Manual entry

"Can't find it? Enter the address manually" replaces the combobox with fields: Street address, Apartment or unit (optional), City, State, ZIP. Submitting calls geocode and shows the candidate list (`address.manualCandidates` "Which of these did you mean?"). Choosing one calls resolve with its `candidateId`. A manual candidate always goes to the confirm screen; it never auto-starts.

### Confirm place

Shown when `matchClass` is `confirm`, after manual entry, off-season for any match class, and (as the interim prompt) when the source mode is `display_only`. A Google-attributed street photo under the default Street View use policy is decorated directly, and a contributor photo is decorated once the visitor has confirmed it (the ordinary confirm screen, with "Yes", the other views and look left and right), so the interim prompt appears only when `STREET_VIEW_USE_POLICY=display_only`, or for contributor imagery when `STREET_VIEW_CONTRIBUTOR_IMAGERY=display_only` ([address spec](address-and-street-view.md#street-view-source-modes)).

Layout, top to bottom:

1. The primary candidate frame on a stage directly under the ink header ([Picture stage](#picture-stage)), laid out at the 1024 by 1024 size its look will have ([Street View frame at its look's size](#street-view-frame-at-its-looks-size); at 1440 by 900 it is 704 px square, not a smaller frame between ink bars), on the vignette ground. It is shown exactly as it is: whole, with no ambient layer, tint or crop, and Google's marks visible and uncovered. While another view loads, a small "Loading this view…" label (`confirm.loadingView`) rests at the frame's top edge, clear of the marks.
2. Directly beneath it on paper, the attribution caption: Google Maps attribution, the per-image copyright string, and the capture date as `confirm.captureDate` "Street View image from {captureDate}" or `confirm.captureDateUnknown` "Street View image, date unknown". When the capture is older than the address spec's threshold, `confirm.oldCapture` "Looks different now? Upload a current photo." appears under the caption.
3. One line: heading `confirm.heading` "Is this the right place?" with the display address under it (shown to the owner on screen only; it never enters a URL, event or log). When the selected view is a `road` view, one more line follows the address: `confirm.roadGuide`; the group of views is described by it, a turned view keeps it because it carries its base view's mode, and it is not shown for `geometry` or `point` views or on the interim prompt. At the end of the heading line sits the primary button `confirm.yes` "Yes, decorate this place" with the secondary `confirm.none` "None of these. Upload a photo instead." as a text link beside it (stacked at full width on phones). Off-season, the holiday picker follows this line (`confirm.chooseHolidayFirst` "Choose a holiday to decorate for.") and the buttons follow the picker; the primary button is disabled until a holiday is chosen and says why, as every disabled control in the product does.
4. Viewpoint adjustment (Open, [D-40](../decisions.md#d-40); default described here): a row of up to 3 candidate thumbnails labeled `confirm.viewN` "View {n}" (for a place on the road the first two thumbnails are the two sides of the road and arrive with their pictures, switching between them needs no request, and the view along the road loads on tap while the display budget allows), and two nudge buttons `confirm.lookLeft` "Look left" and `confirm.lookRight` "Look right". A view that was turned comes back as it was left when its thumbnail is chosen again, so the next turn always starts from the picture on screen. Each nudge fetches a new Static image within the address spec's heading bounds and counts against the per-address Static display budget ([address-and-street-view.md#viewpoint-adjustment](address-and-street-view.md#viewpoint-adjustment)); when the budget is spent the nudges disable and show `confirm.noMoreViews` "No more views are available for this address." An optional small Google map with the address pin and the camera position may sit beside the image if D-40 adopts it; only a Google map may share a screen with Street View imagery (constraint recorded in the address spec).

Confirming calls `PUT /v1/projects/:id/source` with the chosen candidate and then `POST /v1/renders`; the waiting screen follows. In `display_only` mode the stage, attribution and heading are identical, a line `interim.explain` replaces step 4 ("We can show this street photo so you can check the place, but we can't decorate it. Upload a photo of the front and we'll decorate that."; the wording holds for either setting alike), and the primary button becomes the upload action (two buttons on phones, one on desktop, per [Photo upload and capture](#photo-upload-and-capture)). Below the upload action a quiet line `interim.noPhoto` ("Only photos you took or have the right to use can be decorated.") is followed by two secondary links: `interim.changeAddress` ("Try another address", returns to `/`) and `landing.seeExample` ("See an example").

### Auto-start

When `matchClass` is `auto_start` in-window, the client calls `POST /v1/renders` as soon as discovery returns with the bound `sourceAssetId`, and the waiting screen opens with the Street View image visible and the passive link `wait.notYourPlace` "Not your place?" Tapping it cancels the render job and opens the confirm screen with the candidates. Events `source_confirmed` and `render_requested` fire in that order.

### No Street View

One screen: the holiday's hero photograph fills it ([Hero frame](#hero-frame); the header lies over the photograph), and the white dock holds the heading `nosv.heading` "We can't use a street photo for this address", a reason-specific line (with "Try again" for `nosv.error`), the upload step's pickers, the age-and-rights checkbox and the collapsed tips ([Upload screen](#upload-screen)), and `interim.changeAddress` "Try another address". A chosen photo then fills a stage, as on the upload screen. Reason lines: `nosv.no_imagery` "Street View doesn't have a usable photo here."; `nosv.multi_unit` "This looks like a building with several homes, so a street photo can't show your place. Upload a photo of your own space: the entry, balcony or a room."; `nosv.low_precision` "We couldn't pin this address to one building."; `nosv.unavailable` "A street photo isn't available for this address. Upload a photo to decorate it." for the client-visible reason `unavailable` (a blocked address; the exclusion is never disclosed and the screen never claims there is no imagery; rule in [privacy-safety-legal.md#abuse-harassment-and-takedown](privacy-safety-legal.md#abuse-harassment-and-takedown)) and for `out_of_scope`; `nosv.error` "Something went wrong looking up the street photo. You can try again or upload a photo." with a "Try again" secondary action.

### Stale source

When a render answers `SOURCE_NOT_EDITABLE` with reason `stale`, the view behind the place changed since it was confirmed. The client reads the project again and runs discovery again, and discovery decides the screen; the client never starts a render on its own from here. A discovery that finds no imagery (`skip_to_upload`, or `NO_STREET_VIEW`) shows the No Street View screen without the notice. A discovery that finds imagery, whether it would have auto-started or asked for confirmation, shows the confirm screen with `confirm.viewChanged` above the question, because the imagery was replaced and the person has not confirmed it.

### Display-only source

When a render answers `SOURCE_NOT_EDITABLE` with reason `display_only`, the bound street photo may only be displayed ([Street View source modes](address-and-street-view.md#street-view-source-modes)). The client never leaves an empty screen and never starts a render on its own from here:

- A look asked for from a result replaces the result with the upload step, with `error.SOURCE_NOT_EDITABLE` in line (a Street View place's result has no upload control of its own); Cancel on the upload step returns to the result unchanged, with the same preview and the controls on the look shown.
- A page that holds the place's display-only discovery shows the interim photo prompt at once.
- Any other page (the place was confirmed before the setting changed, or the chosen view is one that may only be displayed while the place's primary view can be decorated) reads the project again and runs discovery again. A display-only discovery shows the interim photo prompt; a discovery that offers a view to decorate shows the confirm screen on the primary view with `error.SOURCE_NOT_EDITABLE` in line; a discovery that finds no imagery shows the No Street View screen.

## Photo upload and capture

Interface only. Accepted formats, size limits, client-side preparation, server validation, content admission, people detection and the device test matrix are in [uploads.md](uploads.md).

### Entry points

"Or upload a photo" on the landing page, "Upload a photo instead" while manual entry is open (creates an upload-first project with `POST /v1/projects` carrying the current holiday selection and continues at `/p/:projectId`); "None of these" on the confirm screen; the interim photo prompt; the No Street View screen; "Replace photo" on a result. Inside an existing project the upload replaces the source and starts a new render; the previous variants stay in the project's history. When the upload step was opened from a result, the confirm screen or a render error ("Replace photo", "None of these", "Use another photo"), a quiet `common.cancel` "Cancel" returns to that screen unchanged.

### Upload screen

On its own (`/upload`, the upload step of `/p/:projectId` and the No Street View screen, which puts its heading and reason at the top of the same dock), the step is a picture screen. Until a photo is chosen, the holiday's hero photograph fills the first viewport ([Hero frame](#hero-frame)) with the header laid over it, and items 1 to 6 rest in a white dock (at most 36 rem wide): at the lower left from 600 px (left gutter `clamp(24px, 5vw, 96px)`, bottom gutter `clamp(32px, 7svh, 88px)`), at the top under the header on phones, where the rest of the screen stays photograph. On desktop the picker and its hint share one row and the whole photograph is the drop target (a dim wash with a dashed frame shows while a file is dragged over it). The dock rises in once and does not rise again when the project page takes over from `/upload`; `/upload` shows the same photograph and dock with the heading while it creates the project.

From the moment a photo is chosen (preparing, uploading, checking, held, ready) and for item 7, the photo is shown on a picture stage ([Picture stage](#picture-stage); the person's photo, with its ambient layer from 900 px) under the ink header band, with the 20 px matte from 600 px. A soft sheen crosses it while it is prepared and checked, and a 2 px hairline runs along the stage's lower edge (the upload's own percentage while it uploads). The status line, the scene chip and chooser, messages and buttons follow on paper directly under the stage, lined up with the picture's left edge, the status line inside the first viewport. Inside another screen's column (the interim prompt on the confirm screen) the step keeps its column layout.

1. Heading `upload.heading` "Upload a photo of your place". Subline `upload.subline` "A room, your front door, a balcony or the whole house. Use a photo you took or have permission to use."
2. Pickers. On phones, two pickers, the camera as the solid button and the library, below it, as a quiet text button: `upload.takePhoto` "Take a photo" is a file input with the accept list and `capture="environment"`, which hints the rear camera (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Attributes/capture, retrieved 2026-11-08); `upload.chooseLibrary` "Choose from library" is a plain file input. The two inputs exist because forcing capture removes the library path, and existing photos are the main route for interiors. On desktop, one button `upload.choosePhoto` "Choose a photo" with `upload.dropHint` "or drop a photo here" beside it; on the upload screen the whole photograph (or, once a photo is waiting, the paper under its stage) takes the drop. **Verify:** `capture` behavior on current iOS Safari and Android Chrome against the device matrix in [uploads.md#camera-capture](uploads.md#camera-capture) (owner: web engineer).
3. Guidance, collapsed under `upload.tipsToggle` "Tips for a great photo" (a row with the same chevron as the result's disclosure rows, turned over when open): `upload.tip1` "Step back so the whole wall or front is in the frame.", `upload.tip2` "Hold the phone level, in daylight or bright, even indoor light.", `upload.tip3` "Keep people out of the frame." Guidance content is owned by [uploads.md#scene-type-and-photo-guidance](uploads.md#scene-type-and-photo-guidance); these are its copy keys.
4. Checkbox `upload.attestation` ([D-17](../decisions.md#d-17)): "I'm 18 or older, I have the right to use this photo of this space, and I agree to the Terms." The word "Terms" inside the statement links to the Terms, as in the landing age line; following the link does not toggle the box. The statement version for this wording is `upload-attestation-2026-11b`. The pickers are always enabled; if a file is chosen while the box is unchecked, the file stays on the device, the checkbox receives focus and `upload.attestationRequired` "Please confirm before we upload your photo." appears beside it. Once checked, the attestation holds for the session, so "Replace photo" does not ask again; the record (`attestedAt` and the statement version) is stored per source asset ([data-and-api.md#entities](data-and-api.md#entities)). The whole statement is the checkbox's label and target, at least 44 px tall.
5. Retention line (passive). When `clientConfig.retention.timeBasedDeletion` is true: `upload.retentionLine` "Your photo is private. Unsaved projects are deleted after {anonymousRetention}." with the value from [privacy-safety-legal.md#retention-schedule](privacy-safety-legal.md#retention-schedule) ([D-20](../decisions.md#d-20), Open). Otherwise (the default, and while the value is unknown): `upload.retentionLineKept` "Your photo is private. We keep your looks so you can come back to them."
6. Off-season, or when no holiday has been chosen, the holiday picker appears inline with `upload.chooseHoliday` "Which holiday?" before the pickers.
7. When the project's uploaded photo is attached and ready but nothing has been decorated from it yet (for example its first look was refused with `ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED`, or the screen was reopened off-season), the screen shows that photo on its stage with `result.yourPhoto` "Your photo" and the primary button `upload.continue` "Decorate this photo", which requests its render again; the pickers become secondary and replace the photo. While the allowance snapshot says no looks remain, the photo carries `allowance.bodyNoPreview`, and the screen without an attached photo shows `allowance.uploadNotice` above the pickers, so nobody uploads a photo expecting it to be decorated today. The pickers stay enabled. Replacing a decorated photo ("Replace photo") shows the pickers only until the replacement is attached; from then on the replacement is the photo this item describes, and `common.cancel` returns to the result with the project's newest preview.

### People in the photo (Open, D-18; default Option A)

A sheet shows the photo with each detected region outlined (a 2 px white rounded outline with a thin dark edge, visible on light and dark photos), `upload.peopleHeading` "This photo seems to include people" and `upload.peopleBody` "For their privacy and the best result, crop them out before we decorate." Primary `upload.cropOut` "Crop them out" opens a crop tool whose frame starts positioned to exclude the regions (the whole photo when no crop could be suggested), with free adjustment (each corner handle's 20 px dot sits inside the frame corner, within the frame's 2 px line, so the photo's edge never clips it; its 44 px target reaches 10 px past the corner and is not clipped by the photo, which is clipped on an inner layer together with the frame line and the dimming outside the frame) and a straighten control: a horizontal slider under the photo labeled `upload.straighten` "Straighten" (`role="slider"`, from 15° counterclockwise to 15° clockwise with level in the middle; arrows move 0.5°, Page Up and Page Down 5°, Home and End go to the limits; the whole 44-pixel track is the pointer target; the value reads `upload.straightenLevel` "Level", `upload.straightenClockwise` "{n}° clockwise" or `upload.straightenCounterclockwise` "{n}° counterclockwise"). ### Scene type

After screening, `scene.chip` "Looks like a {sceneTypeLabel} · Change" sits above the photo. Labels for the scene types: `house_exterior` → "house exterior"; `apartment_entry` → "apartment entry"; `balcony` → "balcony or patio"; `interior` → the room type label ("dining room", "living room", "entryway", "kitchen", "bedroom", "room"). Tapping Change lists the four scene types; choosing `interior` then asks the room type. Below the confidence floor ([uploads.md#scene-type-and-photo-guidance](uploads.md#scene-type-and-photo-guidance)) the chip becomes a required choice headed `scene.chooseHeading` "What's in this photo?" before the render request. A required choice marks none of the four types, the detected one included, so it reads as a question: the person taps what the photo shows (and the room type after "A room inside"), and the look starts. For an uploaded photo the same chip sits in the result's More row, and Change offers the same choices. Changing the scene type after a result starts a new render on a new source asset with the same photo, so earlier variants stay in the project's history; what the pipeline reuses is defined in [render-pipeline.md#variant-key-and-reuse](render-pipeline.md#variant-key-and-reuse).

## Waiting and progress

State semantics, transitions and the latency budget are in [render-pipeline.md#lifecycle-state-machines](render-pipeline.md#lifecycle-state-machines) and [render-pipeline.md#latency-budget](render-pipeline.md#latency-budget). This section defines what the screen shows.

### What is on screen

- The source picture on the same stage the result uses ([Picture stage](#picture-stage)), under the ink header, so nothing moves when the result replaces it: an upload at its own size over its blurred ambient layer; a Street View frame as it is (whole, no ambient layer, Google's marks uncovered) at the size its look will have ([Street View frame at its look's size](#street-view-frame-at-its-looks-size)). A slow soft sheen crosses it, fading out before its lower edge, and the light string hangs along the stage's top edge in the colors of the look's holiday ([Light string and status card](#light-string-and-status-card)). Under reduced motion the sheen is absent and the bulbs are lit and still.
- At the look that frames a Street View place (its first look, or the next look of a place whose looks were made before the framing) the job zooms in on the home right after it fetches the photo, and the card shows `stage.framing` with Zoom on the trail after Photo. At the first state the job reports after preparing (analyzing through needs_review), or when it ends, the page reads the project once and shows the zoomed photo it now answers as the place's photo, so the waiting picture is the zoomed one before the result arrives: the new photo loads unseen over the wide one, then fades in over about 400 ms (at once under reduced motion). Both are square frames on the same stage, so nothing moves. If the read fails, or the zoomed photo does not load, the wide photo stays. Uploads read nothing. A page that opened when the job was already past preparing has read the project already and reads nothing more, and one read serves a job whatever the stage it sees first. A page opened later reads the project anyway and shows the zoomed photo from the start; "Before" is the zoomed photo too, except for a look made before the framing, whose "Before" is the view the person confirmed ([data-and-api.md#shared-objects](data-and-api.md#shared-objects)).
- The status card: the label of the stage the job reports, at title size with a pulse beside it; the trail; the time hint `wait.usuallyAbout` "Usually a minute or two"; and Cancel. From 600 px wide on a screen at least 560 px tall it hangs from the string at the top centre of the stage on dark glass. Otherwise it sits on paper directly under the caption, where the title will be, and under 600 px Cancel sits alone, right-aligned, in the row directly under the stage, where the result's Before / AI preview switch will be. While Cancel is being sent the label reads "Canceling…", and Cancel waits until the job exists.
- The trail lists the stages this job has reported, in the order it first reported them, each once with a check, the current one marked and pulsing, by their short words (table below). A stage that repeats (a repair, the check after a relight, the short second planning after a street photo is sharpened) becomes current again and adds nothing. A page opened in the middle of a look also lists the earlier stages its state implies (a job that is decorating has prepared and planned), and never a stage it did not see: Zoom and Sharpen are listed only when the page saw that stage, because those steps do not run at every look (Zoom once per place, Sharpen once per stored frame), and a framing status counts Photo as done. A first evening look of a Street View place therefore reads Photo, Zoom, Plan, Sharpen, Decorate, Check, Evening light. On phones the trail keeps room for two lines and the gap between them, so a trail that wraps moves nothing below it. No percentages, no timers, no stage that was not reported.
- The caption line directly under the stage, on paper: the attribution (Street View) or `result.yourPhoto` "Your photo", and nothing at its end. The screen's heading is `wait.heading` "Decorating your place", for assistive technology and the document title; it is not shown anywhere, because the card states the stage.
- One line of notes on paper, in the title line's place from 600 px when the card is on the stage (with `wait.notYourPlace` at its end after an auto-start) and under the card otherwise: true statements and tips that change every 7 s with a short crossfade, pause while hovered or focused, stop for good once the person uses Next (`wait.nextNote`, a 44 px target shown to everyone), and never change by themselves under reduced motion. The notes are statements about this look and tips, never steps. Which ones show is decided by what is true of the look: the evening and brighter-light notes only for those lightings; the first note says the light is new (`wait.note.keptRelit`) only once the job has reported a light stage (Evening light or Bright light), and says only that the decorations are new (`wait.note.kept`) before that and for a dusk photo decorated in its own light; the sharpen note only once the job has reported that stage; the leave note naming the anonymous retention period only for a place that is not saved while time-based deletion is on (a saved place gets the plain `wait.note.leave`); the save note only for a place not yet saved. The line keeps room for two lines of notes (three under 600 px), so nothing under it moves when a note changes. The notes belong to a place's first look; another look shows none, because the page under the picture does not change.

Cancel works throughout, for a first look and for another look.

A page that resumed the look (after a reload or a link) holds no view to confirm, so it runs discovery again to open the confirm screen with the place's views; it never starts a look by itself, and "Not your place?" leads the same way. A canceled job never publishes an output. A first look canceled from another tab or device leaves the waiting screen exactly as Cancel does, and a page that resumed it runs discovery again for the confirm screen and never starts a look. Cancel is remembered per place for the browser tab's session (a first look canceled with Cancel or "Not your place?", or one that arrives canceled from elsewhere, and only while no preview is on screen): a reload, or a return to that place, does not start the look again. A Street View place opens on the confirm screen (discovery runs with the confirm step first), and an uploaded photo (an upload project, or an address place with an uploaded photo) opens on the upload step with "Decorate this photo". The memory ends when the person starts a look for that place and never reaches another place.
- After `auto_start`, the passive link `wait.notYourPlace` "Not your place?" (see [Auto-start](#auto-start)).
- An examples strip headed `wait.examplesHeading` "While you wait: examples" shows the permissioned before-and-after for the current holiday and scene type first, then the others, each labeled "Example" ([D-11](../decisions.md#d-11)).
- Long wait: after a configured threshold (client `longWaitMs`, default 190 s: above the slowest ordinary first look measured live on 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11, which was about 125 s, and with room for the 30 s a place's first look gains when its street photo is sharpened first) the line `wait.longWait` appears when time-based deletion is on ("This is taking longer than usual. You can keep waiting or come back later; we'll keep your preview here for {anonymousRetention}."), and `wait.longWaitKept` otherwise ("This is taking longer than usual. You can keep waiting or come back later; your preview will be here."), which is also the line for every saved place, because a saved place is kept for its own, longer period and the first line would say otherwise. The line shows on the waiting screen and, for another look that runs past the threshold, in the status card's hint line in place of the time hint (on the band, which has no hint line, in the line under the label in place of the trail), counted from that job's request time. The queue position and the review note keep precedence over it, nothing is added under the picture, and on the band the line is one line with an ellipsis, so on a narrow or short screen the visible text is cut and the full sentence stays in the accessibility tree. The first look's waiting screen keeps the line where it was. The threshold counts from the job's own request time, so a retry never starts with the old job's wait. The time hint `wait.usuallyAbout` "Usually a minute or two" is shown on the status card; looks took 75 to 130 s live on 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11. It is replaced by the queue position while the job waits in line, by the review note in `needs_review`, and, past `longWaitMs`, by the long-wait line of another look. If measured latency leaves that range the string changes ([quality-and-benchmark.md#statistics-and-decision-rules](quality-and-benchmark.md#statistics-and-decision-rules)).

### Leaving and returning

Every project has the route `/p/:projectId`, owned by the session. On load the page calls `GET /v1/projects/:id`, then `GET /v1/renders/:id` for the current job, then subscribes to `GET /v1/renders/:id/events`, with the polling fallback defined in [data-and-api.md#live-updates](data-and-api.md#live-updates). Closing the tab or navigating away does not cancel a render; only Cancel does. The project the address step hands to the page (in the route state, so the page opens without a request) is used once: when the page has opened, it replaces its history entry's state without that project and without the request to open the upload step (`displayAddress`, `entry` and `saved` stay), so a reload, or a return through history, reads the project again and follows the look being made, and never starts a second one or shows the upload step over a running look. The request to open the upload step is honored only for a project with no ready source and no running job. The project page is mounted once per place, so going from one place straight to another (a link, Back or Forward) opens the new place from its own history entry, never from the entry of the place left, and Back between two places shows the place the address bar names. A page that opens just as its first look's job ended superseded with no successor named (the place's photo or view changed from another tab or device) reads the project again, also while it is still on the loading screen, and follows the job the project is running now; with none it leaves the waiting screen as Cancel does (to the confirm screen when nothing runs), it does not stay on "Loading…", and nothing happens if the person left the screen meanwhile. Returning shows the result (within the anonymous retention window while time-based deletion is on; otherwise at any time while the session lasts, 400 days after its last activity). If the session cookie is gone (private window, cleared data) the project cannot be recovered, which is why the result screen offers Save and a share link is the only other durable reference. A `/p/:id` the session does not own, or one that has expired, shows the not-found screen (`notFound.heading` "We couldn't find that" with `notFound.body`).

### Render errors

A look that ends without the picture asked for never opens a sheet or a dialog, and no line of it says "error" or "didn't work out". A person who has waited gets a picture, and the picture on screen stays. When the job ends `rejected`, `failed` or `expired` the page does one of four things, in this order:

1. **The picture the job delivered all the same.** A `rejected` or `failed` status that carries a `variant` shows that picture as the result.
2. **A relit look in the photo's own light.** For an `evening` or `bright` look whose decorations exist in the photo's own light, the page shows that look as the result, on a place's first look and for another look alike, whether or not the person picked the lighting. The picture is the status's `variant`, which the server fills for a rejected `evening` job with the daylight look the job published from its own base ([data-and-api.md#shared-objects](data-and-api.md#shared-objects)), or else the same look as photographed (`source`) of the active photo from the project's looks, so it works whether or not the server fills `variant` (a base held for review, or none, gives `variant` null). One line shows in the notice rail under the picture, `result.eveningUnavailable` "The evening light didn't come out this time, so here is the look in daylight." (a `bright` look: `result.brightUnavailable`), with the action `result.eveningAgain` "Try the evening light again" (`result.brightAgain`), which asks for the same holiday and style in the light that did not arrive. The line goes when a new look starts, another look is shown, or the person dismisses it; dismissing only hides it, and the next look the person picks keeps the evening or bright light it was meant to have. An evening look that could not be made is remembered per project in the tab's session storage, so a reload shows the line again.
3. **One automatic second try.** When nothing could be shown and a second try may cure the reason, the page asks for the same look once more by itself: a rejection by the quality checks or a reviewer, or a failure of the decorating service (`provider_error`, `timeout`, `unknown_outcome`, `internal_error` or no failure kind). The status card stays up with `wait.secondPass` "Taking one more pass to get this right." as its hint (the band's line, for another look). There is one automatic try per look the person asked for; any request the person makes gives it back. Cancel works throughout. A refused photo (`source_rejected`, `source_unavailable`, `moderation_blocked`, `policy_denied`) and an expired job (the session or project expired, which a retry does not fix) get no second try. A first look that ended while the page was loading over an existing preview makes its second try as another look on that result. If the second try's request itself fails (rate limit, budget pause, allowance), the person gets the notice of step 4, not an inline error.
4. **One calm notice.** `result.lookUnfinished` "We couldn't finish this look. Nothing was counted against your looks today." with `common.tryAgain` "Try again" as the primary action and `common.useAnotherPhoto` "Use another photo" beside it, from the one component `UnfinishedLook`. For another look it sits in the rail under the current preview, which stays; for a first look it sits on the place's photo under the heading "Your place" (the page title is "Your place", or the look's title when a preview is on screen). Failed jobs do not count toward the allowance, so the second sentence is true ([data-and-api.md#allowance-quotas-and-rate-limits](data-and-api.md#allowance-quotas-and-rate-limits)). `error.RENDER_REJECTED` reads "We couldn't finish this look. Try again, or use another photo."

The server makes steps 1 and 2 likelier: a verdict that is only a doubt about a detail of the home is delivered when no repair can follow, and an accepted doubted base is published as the daylight look when its relight does not arrive ([render-pipeline.md#decision-policy](render-pipeline.md#decision-policy), [render-pipeline.md#repair-and-retries](render-pipeline.md#repair-and-retries)). A look that failed the checks with no repair left is also delivered, when every spot the judge objected to can be pasted back from the original and enough of the decorations remain: the picture is then an accepted doubt, with the home exactly as photographed at those spots and the decorations there gone, and it is delivered like any accepted doubt (as the look asked for, or, for an evening look whose relight does not arrive, as the daylight look of steps 1 and 2); a picture of another home, a moved camera, unsafe content or an altered attribution is never delivered this way.

A photo that cannot be decorated keeps its specific message and actions in the same inline notice, on the place's photo or under the current preview, with no automatic second try and no "Try again":

| Outcome | Message | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `failed` with `failureKind` `provider_error`, `timeout`, `unknown_outcome` or `internal_error`, after the second try | `result.lookUnfinished` | "Try again" (a new render job with the same selection); "Use another photo" |
| `rejected` (`rejectionReason` `qa_critical`, `qa_uncertain` or `reviewer_rejected`) with no picture to show, after the second try | `result.lookUnfinished` | "Try again"; "Use another photo" |
| `expired` | `result.lookUnfinished` | "Try again"; "Use another photo" |
| `failed` with `source_unavailable`, Street View origin | `wait.sourceUnavailable` "The street photo of this place is no longer available. | `failed` with `source_unavailable`, upload origin | `wait.sourceUnavailableUpload` "Your photo is no longer available. Upload it again to continue." | "Use another photo" |

| `failed` with `source_rejected`, `failureDetail` `house_number_mismatch` | `wait.wrongHouse` "This street photo may not show the right house. Choose another view or upload a photo." | "Use another view" and "Use another photo" |
| `failed` with `source_rejected`, `failureDetail` `owner_blurred` | `wait.ownerBlurred` "This home's Street View image is blurred at the owner's request. Upload your own photo to see it decorated." | "Use another photo" only; alternates are not offered ([address-and-street-view.md#panorama-changes-blur-and-takedown](address-and-street-view.md#panorama-changes-blur-and-takedown)) |

How the page behaves around these endings:

- **The first-look notice is remembered for the tab.** A first look that ends in its notice with no preview on screen is remembered for the browser tab, as a canceled first look is. A reload, the Gallery and back, or opening the place again in that tab opens the place's view to confirm (or the uploaded photo with "Decorate this photo") and starts nothing. The memory ends once the server accepts a look the person asks for; a refused request leaves it in place.
- **The second try is remembered per place.** The job of the page's automatic second try is remembered per place for the tab. After a reload that job keeps the hint `wait.secondPass` on its status card. If it ends without a picture the page shows the notice and never asks a third time.
- **"Use another view" never decorates the same view again.** On a notice it opens the views the page holds; with none, it runs discovery for the confirm screen.
- **The looks are read again.** Whenever a look in the making ends without a new picture (canceled here or elsewhere, replaced with no successor, or ended in the notice), the page reads the place's looks again. Every look it holds, the one on screen included, then carries the "Before" the server gives now. The daylight fallback reads them as it shows the look.
- **A refused request shows above the notice.** The first-look notice shows the inline message of a refused request (rate limit, network, expired project) above the notice, with the timed "Try again" for a rate limit.
- **A tap on the selected control starts nothing.** A tap on a control that is already selected for the look on screen starts nothing, also while a daylight look stands in for an evening or bright look. With a notice open, it asks for that look again.
- **Leaving during the second try's request.** "Not your place?" or Cancel while the second try's request is on its way stays on the screen it led to. The job that request created is canceled when its answer arrives, and a refusal that arrives later shows nothing.
- **The status region.** The page's polite status region says the second try's hint and, once per job, the notice of a look that could not be finished (in the photo's own words). It also says the line under a look shown in the photo's own light, including when that line appears as a place opens.
- **The evening line in any tab.** A place opened where its remembered look is evening or bright (still offered for its holiday), with no look of that holiday and style in that light but with the same look as photographed, and no running job, opens on that as-photographed look. It shows the line `result.eveningUnavailable` or `result.brightUnavailable` and its action, in any tab. Dismiss hides the line, and the next look keeps the light.
- **No clear view from the street.** A Street View look that fails `source_rejected` with `facade_not_in_frame` shows `wait.noClearView` with "Use another view" and "Use another photo"; an uploaded photo with that detail keeps `wait.nothingToDecorate`. `wait.noClearView` can arrive within seconds, while the label reads "Zooming in on your home", for any view from the road in which one home cannot be settled; that view is not declined, so asking for it again runs the step again and the confirm screen never answers that there is no street photo for it.

"Show it as photographed" no longer exists: a relit look's daylight picture is shown as the result without being asked for. Whether a failed or rejected job consumes the allowance is defined in [data-and-api.md#allowance-quotas-and-rate-limits](data-and-api.md#allowance-quotas-and-rate-limits); the copy above states only what the person can do next.

## Result and comparison

### Result card

The picture is the first viewport. It sits on a picture stage directly under the ink header ([Picture stage](#picture-stage)): the full width of the viewport, with no frame, border or gutter, the picture contained and centered (never cropped, never above its natural size) and, from 900 px, its own rendering repeated behind it as the ambient layer ([Ambient layer](#ambient-layer)). On a 1440 by 900 screen a square picture is 704 px tall and a 3:2 picture 1056 px wide; on a phone it is the full width of the screen. Before and after are always displayed at the same rendered size (sizes in [render-pipeline.md#compositing-and-resolution](render-pipeline.md#compositing-and-resolution)). Under the stage, on paper in one column as wide as the picture and at least 55 rem (never wider than the screen less its gutters), come the caption line and the title line with the action row at its end. The first viewport shows the stage, the caption line and the title line with its actions.

- Before / AI preview switch: a two-button segmented control, `result.before` "Before" and `result.aiPreview` "AI preview", with `aria-pressed`, drawn as a dark glass pill in the stage's upper right corner from 600 px, never over the picture's lower corners (under 600 px it sits on paper under the picture, [Phones](#phones-the-picture-carries-nothing-but-its-marks)). Tapping "Before" shows the source image fully; tapping "AI preview" shows the accepted variant fully. The default on arrival is "AI preview".
- Reveal: see [Reveal](#reveal).
- Comparison slider on the image: a vertical divider whose handle is a `role="slider"` element; the value is the percentage of the width, from the left, that shows the before image (0 = all preview, 100 = all before). Arrow keys move 5, Page Up and Page Down move 20, Home sets 0 and End sets 100; `aria-valuetext` reads "{n}% before photo". The handle is at least 44 CSS pixels in each dimension and uses `touch-action: pan-y` so a page can still scroll. The slider is an enhancement; the toggle is always present (requirement in [Accessibility](#accessibility)).
- "AI preview" badge: persistent in a corner of the generated pixels, never over the attribution strip or attribution burned into the image ([D-28](../decisions.md#d-28)); which corner follows [AI preview badge placement](#ai-preview-badge-placement).
- Caption line directly beneath the stage, on paper: for Street View origin, the Google Maps attribution, per-image copyright and capture date per [address-and-street-view.md#before-image-and-attribution](address-and-street-view.md#before-image-and-attribution), present in every toggle state and slider position; for uploads, `result.yourPhoto` "Your photo". From 600 px the look's label sits at the line's end in 11 px uppercase with 0.16em tracking: `result.holidayLabel` "{holiday} · {style} · {lighting}", or `result.holidayStyleLabel` "{holiday} · {style}" for a `bright` look or when the scene offers a single lighting mode that the look uses. The client keeps each part of the label whole (non-breaking spaces inside "As photographed" or "New Year's Eve") and puts a non-breaking space before each separator, so a narrow label wraps only after a separator; the copy-deck rows are unchanged, the joins are applied in code. The same rule applies to the "Try another look" summary and to gallery captions; share captions name only the holiday and the style. Under 600 px the label is not shown beside the switch: the title names the holiday and the "Try another look" row names the look.
- Title line: the title ("Your place, ready for Christmas") at the start and the action row at the end ([Below the card](#below-the-card)).
- `compare_used` fires once per variant when the toggle or slider is first used; `variant_viewed` fires each time a variant is displayed.

### Reveal

When a look arrives while the person is watching (the waiting screen to the first result, or a pending look to its result), the picture first shows the earlier picture (the source photo for a first look, the look it replaces for a pending one) and holds for about 0.7 s once the new picture has loaded. A white divider then sweeps once from the right edge to the left over `--dur-reveal` (1 s) and uncovers the new look, and the slider rests at 0 (AI preview). The AI preview badge and the slider's handle arrive after the sweep; the title line rises in at 1.5 s and the rows at 1.8 s. The reveal plays once: there is no sweep on a revisit or for a look chosen from those already made (a short crossfade of 560 ms instead), and under reduced motion the picture swaps at once and the title line and rows appear at once.

### AI preview badge placement

The on-screen "AI preview" badge goes in the lower-right corner of the image, except on Street View-origin images. A live Google frame carries the Google logo burned into its lower-left corner and the copyright into its lower-right corner (the measured rectangles in [address-and-street-view.md#before-image-and-attribution](address-and-street-view.md#before-image-and-attribution)), and a fixture stand-in carries its credit at the lower right, so on a Street View-origin image the badge goes in the upper-left corner, where nothing of Hailiday's covers either attribution rectangle. Upload-origin images and the example library carry no burned-in attribution and keep the lower-right corner. An image is Street View-origin when its payload carries Street View attribution (`Variant.attribution`, `ShareView.attribution`) or, for a variant made from the project's active source, when that source is a Street View or fixture Street View frame.

The rule applies everywhere the badge appears: the result card, the share page (server-rendered and in the app), the share card preview in the Share sheet, Gallery tiles and the gallery viewer, Your places thumbnails and the examples (example art is never Street View-origin and keeps the lower right). On the result card the badge belongs to the generated layer and is clipped together with the generated pixels, so it never labels the before photo. The lower-right badge of an upload stays pinned to its corner, so the before photo covers it as the divider passes over it. The upper-left badge of a Street View frame rides along the top edge 12 px to the right of the divider, so it stays on the generated pixels for as long as any of them show (near the right edge the frame clips it). The badge never moves onto burned-in attribution, which holds both lower corners. In fixture mode the development "Fixture" badge of a Street View frame stays at the upper right (one badge for whichever layer is fixture evidence), so the upper-left corner belongs to the AI preview badge. Placement is a class on the badge's corner element (`--lower-right` or `--upper-left`), so component tests assert it per origin without screenshots. The burned-in download mark and the share card mark follow the same rule and are placed by the render pipeline ([render-pipeline.md#provenance-and-labeling](render-pipeline.md#provenance-and-labeling)).

### Below the card

The result page is minimal: the picture first, then one compact action row, then the rows. Everything except the picture, the title line and the action row sits in collapsed disclosure rows the person opens when they want them. The rows share the column under the stage (as wide as the picture, at least 55 rem, never wider than the screen less its gutters), and running text inside them stays at most 760 px wide; there is no side rail.

1. Action row, at the end of the title line under the picture (it wraps under the title on phones, with 16 px gaps under 420 px): `result.save` "Save" is the only primary button (`result.saved` "Saved" once saved). `result.share` "Share", `result.download` "Download" (only when permitted; see [Downloads](#downloads)) and a Gallery link (`gallery.title`) are quiet text links, each at least 44 by 44 px. There is no overflow menu: Delete lives in the More row, and Replace photo, Change address and Delete are 44 px targets too.
2. Notices, each one line led by a small quiet dot and only when true: `result.privateOnly`; `result.existingDecor` "This photo already had decorations. We kept them and added the {holiday} look." ## Changing holiday, style and lighting

The controls live inside the collapsed "Try another look" row under the result card ([Below the card](#below-the-card)); the person opens it to change a look. The controls are enabled except while a look is being made. Then they are locked ([below](#while-a-look-is-being-made)); otherwise what a tap does depends on the state below.

| Control | Values and labels | Default | Shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Holiday chip | Supported holidays by display name, in the picker order from [holidays-and-content.md#windows-and-selection](holidays-and-content.md#windows-and-selection) | The window default, or the person's deliberate choice | Always |
| Style | `subtle` "Subtle", `classic` "Classic", `abundant` "Abundant" | `abundant` in every pack and scene type (the project's `offered.defaultStyle`; [D-10](../decisions.md#d-10)) | After the first result; the shown values are the project's `offered.styles` |
| Lighting | `source` "As photographed", `evening` "Evening", `bright` "Bright" | The project's `offered.defaultLightingMode`: `evening` for `house_exterior` and `balcony`, unless the server's filters removed it (a display-only Street View source, or a photo the analysis judged not daylight), in which case `source`; `bright` for `interior` and `apartment_entry` ([D-10](../decisions.md#d-10)) | After the first result, and only when the project's `offered.lightingModes` (from `GET /v1/projects/:id/variants`; computed by the server from the pack's lighting policy ([holidays-and-content.md#lighting-policy](holidays-and-content.md#lighting-policy)), the source-kind configuration and whether the source was judged daylight, [render-pipeline.md#lighting-modes](render-pipeline.md#lighting-modes)) includes `evening`; otherwise the control is absent, not disabled. `bright` is the only lighting of `interior` and `apartment_entry`, so no lighting control is shown there and no caption names it |

The client never offers a combination outside the offered sets; the server refuses one with `VALIDATION_FAILED`. The first look of a project comes from the server: the project's `offered.defaultStyle` and `offered.defaultLightingMode`, which the server also stores on the project whenever the scene type is set or changes (a look the person chose survives a rebind that keeps the scene type). A render the person never chose a look for uses those defaults before the first preview and the remembered look afterwards (kept inside what the holiday offers); "Try the evening light again" asks for the same holiday and style in that light; "Try again" repeats the failed job's style and lighting for the holiday selected now; correcting the scene type renders the new scene type's default look.

When an `evening` or `bright` look ends `rejected` or `failed` and its decorations exist in the photo's own light, the result shows that look as photographed whether or not the person picked the lighting, with the line and the action of [Render errors](#render-errors) and no error: the picture is the status's variant or the look as photographed of the active photo. The action asks for the same holiday and style in the light that did not arrive. Coming back to an evening look that could not be made in the same visit (also after a reload: the browser keeps the list per project in session storage) opens the as-photographed variant again with the same line, without a request, until the person asks for the evening light again. In another tab the same holds when the project's remembered look is evening or bright and still offered, no look of that holiday and style exists in that light, the as-photographed look does, and no job is running ([Render errors](#render-errors)). After the first result of a dusk or night photo, evening is no longer offered, and an as-photographed look of the same holiday and style whose lighting the person did not pick opens that photo's evening variant (decorated in its own light) instead of requesting a new render. A `bright` job whose brightening does not arrive delivers its decorated base as the result ([render-pipeline.md#repair-and-retries](render-pipeline.md#repair-and-retries)), so the brighter-light line is rare.

Behavior on a tap:

1. If the project already has an accepted variant for the chosen combination (`GET /v1/projects/:id/variants`), the picture switches to it instantly. No request is sent and nothing is consumed; `variant_viewed` fires.
2. Otherwise the page marks the look as being made, so the controls lock at the tap, and sends `POST /v1/renders` with an `Idempotency-Key` at once. Before it, the page reads the project's looks and its running job: a look another tab or device is already making is joined instead of replaced, and a look made elsewhere since this page loaded opens without a request. A 200 returns an existing accepted variant and the picture switches instantly; a 202 shows the status card and the light string. There is no debounce: a second choice cannot be made while the first is being made ([render-pipeline.md#superseding-and-coalescing-rapid-switches](render-pipeline.md#superseding-and-coalescing-rapid-switches)).
3. If the allowance is exhausted, the allowance sheet opens instead of a request (the client knows from the allowance snapshot; the server remains authoritative and returns `ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED` if they disagree).

"Change address" returns to `/` with the address field focused and the holiday preserved; the new address is a new project, and the previous one remains in the session's history (for the retention window while time-based deletion is on, and otherwise until deleted); typing the same address again returns to the existing project and its result instead of creating another. "Replace photo" opens the upload screen inside the project.

### While a look is being made

One look is made at a time. From the tap that starts a look until it arrives, fails or is canceled, every control that would start another look or change the place is locked:

- the holiday, style and lighting controls in "Try another look", the header's holiday picker (it does not open and the app's selected holiday does not change), the scene chip in More, Replace photo, Change address and Delete;
- on a place's first look, the same holiday picker and any action that would start a render. The actions of a failed look ("Try again" and the others) appear only once the look has ended.

A locked control is dimmed (the choice being made keeps its fill), still focusable, `aria-disabled` and inert, and described by `controls.locked` "You can pick another look when this one is ready." Using one changes nothing, says the line in the page's polite status and shows it on the status card for 4 s. The "Try another look" row shows the same line in place of the allowance counter for as long as the lock lasts. Save, Share, Download, Gallery, the comparison, Shop the look and feedback act on the look on screen and stay available. Cancel stops a look, and so does "Not your place?" on an auto-started first look; both are deliberate.

The lock is held by the page's own code, not only by its buttons: a keyboard press, a header choice or a request already on its way starts nothing while a look is being made. A look that differs from the one being made in two choices therefore takes two looks, or a Cancel and a new choice; this is the accepted cost of never interrupting a look by accident (a replaced job's paid attempt may still bill, [render-pipeline.md#superseding-and-coalescing-rapid-switches](render-pipeline.md#superseding-and-coalescing-rapid-switches)). A request from another tab or device still supersedes the job; the page follows the newer one, and a narrow race remains that only the server can close.

Inside the "Try another look" row, `allowance.counter` "{n} more looks today" (`allowance.counterOne` "1 more look today" for the last one) is a status line with a small dot, green while looks remain and the separator gray at zero, with muted text. It is fed by the allowance snapshot in `GET /v1/experience` and updated from each render response. At zero it reads `allowance.counterZero` "No more looks today · more at {resetsAt}". ## Allowance and limits

Five codes stop or delay a paid action. Each has its own screen so a person never confuses their own limit with a service condition. Enforcement points, payload fields and `Retry-After` semantics are in [data-and-api.md#allowance-quotas-and-rate-limits](data-and-api.md#allowance-quotas-and-rate-limits).

| Code | Where it can appear | What the person sees | What stays usable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ALLOWANCE_EXHAUSTED` | `POST /v1/renders` (looks); `POST /v1/addresses/resolve` (new addresses) | The allowance sheet ([Open, D-13](../decisions.md#d-13); pilot default below) | Everything: the current result, comparison, cached variants, Save, Share, Download, Delete |
| `RATE_LIMITED` | Suggestions, renders, uploads, reports; session start (`POST /v1/sessions`) | Suggestions: silent retry after `Retry-After`, then `error.RATE_LIMITED` inline if it persists. Other actions: `error.RATE_LIMITED` inline with a "Try again" that enables after the wait. Session start: `session.limited` at once wherever the action was attempted, with a "Try again" that enables after the wait ([Starting the session](#starting-the-session)) | Everything else |
| `BUDGET_PAUSED` | Renders, discovery | Banner `error.BUDGET_PAUSED` at the top of `/` and `/p/:id`; a tap on a control repeats it inline. No retry loop and no countdown. | Viewing, comparing, saving, sharing, deleting |
| `AT_CAPACITY` | Renders | Sheet `error.AT_CAPACITY` with `common.tryAgain` enabled after the `Retry-After` countdown. The request is not queued automatically. | Everything else |
| `VERIFICATION_REQUIRED` | Any paid action when the bot check demands it | The challenge widget inline where the action was attempted; on success the action is retried once | Everything else |

### Starting the session

Every call except the public share read and the analytics batch waits for the session (`POST /v1/sessions`, created on the first interaction or the first call that needs it). Analytics never starts it ([Screen and state inventory](#screen-and-state-inventory)), so choosing a holiday on the landing page sends no request and runs no bot check. Starting it never leaves a flow waiting silently:

- The bot-check script and the session request each have a deadline (15 seconds); past it the start counts as failed.
- A failed start fails every call that was waiting on it at once, marked as a session failure, and each flow shows it where the action was attempted: under the address field (suggestions, resolve, manual entry), on `/upload`, on the upload screen, on the project page and in sheets. The line names the session, never the person's input: `session.limited` when the start was refused with `RATE_LIMITED` (its `{retryAfter}` is the remaining `Retry-After`; a 429 without the JSON error envelope, such as a plain-text or HTML answer from a proxy, counts as `RATE_LIMITED` with its `Retry-After`), `session.failed` for anything else (network, timeout, server error). A refused bot check keeps `error.VERIFICATION_REQUIRED`.
- Each place offers `common.tryAgain`, enabled once the `Retry-After` wait has passed (at once for other failures); it repeats the action, which starts the session again. Under the address field it repeats the last lookup with the typed text kept.
- After a `RATE_LIMITED` refusal the client sends no new session request until `Retry-After` has passed (30 seconds when the response names none): calls made during the wait fail at once with `session.limited` and the remaining wait, so no flow hangs and the limit is not hammered. Suggestions do not apply their silent retry to a session refusal.

A preview of the share card (the image generated for link previews; size and generation in [../architecture.md#landing-and-share-page-delivery](../architecture.md#landing-and-share-page-delivery)) with `share.cardNote` "This is what people see when you share the link. It never includes your address."
2. Option `share.includeBefore` "Include the before photo" appears only for upload-origin variants that are not private-only; off by default. Street View-origin shares never include the before image, on the share page or the share card, in the US Release: the unedited Street View source is not exported ([address-and-street-view.md#licensing-request-brief](address-and-street-view.md#licensing-request-brief), row 8). Their share page and card show only the after image, with attribution, where `distributeDerivative` permits.
3. Expiry line `share.expiry` "The link works for {shareLifetime}." (default 30 days, configuration).
4. Buttons: `share.copyLink` "Copy link" and, where the browser offers it, `share.shareVia` "Share…" using the native share sheet.
5. For an existing share: the link, its expiry, and `share.stop` "Stop sharing" with the confirmation `share.stopConfirm` "Stop sharing? People with the link will no longer see it. Copies already saved can't be recalled."

Creating a share calls `POST /v1/shares`; stopping calls `DELETE /v1/shares/:token`. A private-only variant (people kept in the photo) has no Share or Download action, and the sheet never appears for it. Street View-origin variants can be shared only when the evaluated permission allows `distributeDerivative` ([address-and-street-view.md#licensing-outcomes-and-activation](address-and-street-view.md#licensing-outcomes-and-activation)); otherwise the Share action is absent and `result.shareUnavailable` "Sharing isn't available for this street photo." appears in the More row in its place. Under the default `full` Street View use policy `distributeDerivative` is allowed, so Street View-origin results can be shared and downloaded (with the attribution band); under `decorate` and `display_only` they cannot.

### Share page

`GET /s/:token` is server-rendered, `noindex`, and contains, top to bottom:

- the brand at the top left, on an ink header band (the `stage` tone: the header on `--stage` with white type, `theme-color` `#0d0d0f`, in the server-rendered page and in the app);
- the after image on a picture stage directly under the header ([Picture stage](#picture-stage)): the full width of the viewport on the ink ground, the picture contained at the largest size the first viewport allows under the header less the stage peek (never above its natural size, never cropped), with its blurred, darkened ambient copy behind it from 900 px, the 20 px matte under it from 600 px, and the "AI preview" badge (upper-left on Street View-derived images, lower-right otherwise; [AI preview badge placement](#ai-preview-badge-placement)); there is no card, frame, border or shadow around the picture;
- the comparison toggle, only when the share includes the before image (upload-origin only): from 600 px a dark glass segmented pill in the stage's upper right corner; under 600 px a paper segmented control on the paper directly under the picture, right-aligned, so nothing but the AI preview mark lies on the picture (the page carries both and the stylesheet shows one; on phones the control has the first row under the picture to itself, the look's line follows it, and the stage reserves a 248 px peek);
- then, on the paper directly beneath the stage and lined up with the picture's edges (never narrower than 54 rem on wide screens; the screen width less the gutters on phones): the holiday label (uppercase micro-label) and, for Street View-derived images, the attribution (required and permitted per the address spec) on one line; `share.caption` "A holiday preview made with Hailiday" as the page's `h1`, on one row with the call to action `share.cta` "See your place for the holidays" linking to `/` with the holiday preselected (stacked on phones), both inside the first viewport at every size from 320 by 568 to 1920 by 1080;
- `share.report` "Report this image" as a quiet text link (44 px tall), and the footer.

The page shows no address, no map, no name, and no product links or disclosure ([D-26](../decisions.md#d-26)). `share_viewed` fires on load without viewer identity. A revoked or expired token returns a paper page (white header, `theme-color` `#ffffff`) with `error.SHARE_REVOKED` as one centered line and the call to action only. The server paints the page with the markup and classes of the app's share page (their styles ship in the page's stylesheet), so it is laid out and styled before any script runs and does not move when the app takes over. The picture's arrival (a 1.2 s fade in from 1.5% larger) plays once, on the server-painted page; the app's picture replaces it in place and continues that arrival from the time since the page first painted instead of playing it again. Under 600 px the share page reserves a 184 px peek under the stage (instead of 88 px), so a tall portrait still leaves the headline and the button in the first viewport.

### Downloads

"Download" appears only when the `Variant` payload carries `downloadUrl` ([data-and-api.md#http-api](data-and-api.md#http-api)); the client never decides eligibility itself. The server includes the field only when the variant is `downloadable` ([D-22](../decisions.md#d-22)): upload-origin variants that are not private-only, and Street View-origin variants only when the evaluated permission allows `distributeDerivative` (the default `full` use policy does; a recorded grant may not). The file is produced when the variant is accepted: the delivered after image with a small burned-in "AI preview" corner mark (on by default; configuration, [D-28](../decisions.md#d-28)), IPTC digital-source-type metadata and, for Street View-origin variants where distribution is permitted, the required attribution band ([render-pipeline.md#provenance-and-labeling](render-pipeline.md#provenance-and-labeling)). `downloadUrl` is signed and short-lived, so the client re-reads the project's variants for a fresh link when it has expired (the result page's Download and the gallery viewer's both do), and follows the fresh link in the window the click opened, or in the same tab when the browser blocked that window. The first download in a session shows `result.downloadNote` "Downloads include the AI preview label." once.

### Delete

`result.delete` (in the More row) opens `delete.confirm` "Delete this place? This removes the photo, every preview and any share links. Downloaded copies can't be recalled." with `delete.yes` "Delete" and `common.cancel` "Cancel". `DELETE /v1/projects/:id` runs, `delete.done` "Deleted." shows, and the person returns to `/`.

### Legal, information and not-found pages

Each legal page is a document column (720 px): a small tracked "Draft" kicker with a quiet dot, the title as the display title, the draft notice as a small muted line, and the plain-language summary in a framed note (hairline, 10 px radius, light 14 px text). Copy keys, the privacy retention summary and the `#report` anchor are unchanged. The not-found page is the season's hero photograph edge to edge with the header laid over it ([Hero frame](#hero-frame)); a white dock (at the lower left from 600 px, at the top under the header on phones, at most 32 rem wide) holds the heading (display weight, focused on arrival), `notFound.body` and "Back to the start" as one solid action at its own width. The dock rises in once.

### Homeowner opt-out

`/dont-decorate-my-home`, linked from every footer as `footer.optOut` "Don't decorate my home". Heading `optout.heading` "Don't decorate my home". Body `optout.body` "Tell us the address and we'll stop showing a street photo of it. People can still upload their own photos of places they have the right to use." The form uses the same address combobox, a contact email for verification, and `optout.submit` "Send request". It calls `POST /v1/reports` with reason `this_is_my_home` and the resolved place; the resulting list entry routes future discoveries to `skip_to_upload` with reason `blocked_address`. Confirmation `optout.thanks` "Thanks. We'll confirm by email once the address is on our list." The page also notes that homeowners can ask Google to blur the house at the source (`optout.googleNote` "To blur your home in Google Street View itself, use Google's Report a problem tool."). Every field label matches the address field's small uppercase label, and the submit action keeps its own width.

## Copy deck

Every user-facing string has a key. Keys are stable identifiers used by the web app and by the error table in [data-and-api.md#error-codes](data-and-api.md#error-codes) (`error.<CODE>`). Strings are US English for the US Release and are kept in a message catalog, never hard-coded. Placeholders in braces are filled at runtime from configuration or API fields. Two customer-facing words are fixed: "preview" is an accepted variant shown with its label, and "look" is one combination of holiday, style and lighting. Disclosure wording for Shop the look is owned by [commerce.md#disclosure](commerce.md#disclosure) and is referenced, not copied, here. A row that lists several keys separated by " / ", or several values separated by " · ", defines one catalog entry per value in the same order.

## Accessibility

The conformance target is WCAG 2.2 Level AA (NFR-06; https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/, retrieved 2026-11-08) for every public page and the complete address, upload, result, sharing and account flows. The public statement lives in [privacy-safety-legal.md#accessibility-statement](privacy-safety-legal.md#accessibility-statement); the automated checks run in CI as defined in [../architecture.md#continuous-integration-and-testing](../architecture.md#continuous-integration-and-testing).

### Focus and announcements

- Every route sets the document title "{page} · Hailiday" (`pageTitle.template`; the landing page is "Hailiday"). The page name is the screen's heading or a `pageTitle.*` key: Your places, Your account, Sign in, the legal page titles, Don't decorate my home, A holiday preview (share page), and on `/p/:id` the heading of the current screen (confirm, waiting, result, render error, upload, No Street View) or "Your place" while loading. A title never contains an address.
- After a client-side navigation a polite live region announces the new document title, and focus moves to the page's `h1` (made focusable with `tabindex="-1"`), or to the `main` region when the page has no heading yet. A page that has already placed focus inside its main region (the result heading, an autofocused address field) or in an open sheet keeps it. The first page load is left to the browser.
- A look has one polite status. It says the start (`wait.heading` or `result.decoratingLook`), each stage label once when it changes (a stage that repeats after another is said again; the same label twice in a row is not), the hint `wait.secondPass` while the second try runs, once per job the notice of a look that ended without a picture, the line under a look shown in the photo's own light (also when it appears as a place opens), the lock line when a locked control is used and, for another look, the new title when it arrives. An ended look's outcome is cleared after 4 s, like the arrival title. The visible label, the trail, the time hint and the rotating note are not live; a note is announced only when the person asked for it with Next. Focus does not move when a look starts or when another look arrives; on a first look it moves to the result's heading when the look arrives. On the upload screen the live region names the stage only ("Uploading your photo…", `upload.uploadingStage`); the byte percentage and its bar are visual only. Countdowns (the at-capacity wait) are not live; the sheet's label carries the wait and "You can try again now." (`capacity.ready`) is announced once when it ends.
- When a result arrives, focus moves to the result heading; when a render fails, focus moves to the error heading; after an upload is declined, focus moves to the message and "Try another photo" is next in the tab order.
- Errors are identified in text next to the control they concern and are also announced.
- No content is conveyed by color alone; the current comparison state is also in the pressed button text.

### Visual and motion

- Text on paper meets 4.5:1 contrast (3:1 for large text) at every viewport. The status card's white type on its glass is 10.0:1 and its secondary type 6.1:1 over a white picture; locked controls are inactive components, and the pressed choice keeps full contrast. Nothing in the wait depends on color alone: the current trail entry differs by its dot and its "in progress" text. Over a photograph, small white text sits on a dark glass pill (the eyebrow, the holiday chip, the AI preview mark, the continue pill) or in the header band, at least 4.5:1 against the worst pixel behind it. The landing headline (large bold type) sits on its own scrim (a band on phones, one soft ellipse from 600 px) and a soft ink shadow ([Scrims](#scrims)): measured in Chromium behind each line, with the photograph's drift at its start and at its end, for all five hero photographs (the four holidays and the neutral one) at 1920×1080, 1440×900, 1366×768, 1280×720, 1024×768, 768×1024, 390×844, 360×640 and 320×568, the median is at least 4.5:1, the 5th percentile at least 3:1 and no pixel under 2:1. All other text on a hero frame is in the white dock. Scrims and gradients are mixed only from `--scrim-rgb`.
- White header type over a hero photograph (the upload entry, the not-found page, the No Street View screen, the landing) meets 4.5:1 at the brightest pixel behind it for all five heroes at the sizes above; on phones the upload entry, not-found and No Street View frames add a band of `rgb(var(--scrim-rgb) / 0.24)` behind the header that fades out 32 px below it. On a stage the header sits on its own ink band, so its type needs no scrim.
- The boundary of every text field, select, the address field and the upload drop target uses `--control-border` (#86868b), at least 3:1 against white, #fafbfc and #f2f3f5 (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.11). Text buttons and chips keep the quieter line; their text identifies them.
- Small meaningful text uses #6e6e73 or darker (4.5:1 on every surface). The decorative grays #86868b, #a8adb5 and #c7cbd0 are never used for essential text.
- Marks on photographs (AI preview, Example, Fixture) are white on `--glass-dark`, at least 4.5:1 for their text over white; the Before / AI preview switch on a stage is white on a 0.66 ink glass, at least 4.5:1 even over a white picture. On paper the focus ring is a 2 px ink outline offset 3 px (fields: an ink border plus `0 0 0 3px #fff, 0 0 0 5px #1d1d1f`). Rows of a list that clips to its rounded frame (the holiday picker, the manual-entry candidate list) draw the outline inside the row (offset -2 px). Gallery tiles and Your places cards draw a 2 px ink outline 1 px outside the tile, in the 4 px gap, never over the picture. On a picture surface the ring is white with an ink edge: on a hero frame and on the ink header a 2 px white outline with a 4 px ink edge, on a stage a 3 px white ring with a 2 px ink edge, and in the gallery viewer a 2 px white outline offset 3 px. Wherever the ring is drawn as a shadow (text fields, the address field, the disclosure rows, the comparison handle), the element also carries a 2 px transparent outline, which forced-colors mode draws in the system color. Every form label uses the address field's label style: 11 px, weight 500, uppercase, 0.16em tracking, #6e6e73.
- The landing entrance: the photograph paints at once, and the type and the dock rise in over it in a staggered rise (about 1 s, `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`); the hero photograph drifts once from scale 1 to 1.06 over 24 s.
- Targets are at least 24 by 24 CSS pixels (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum), Level AA; https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html, retrieved 2026-11-08); primary controls and the slider handle are at least 44 by 44, and the controls of the picture screens (landing, result, waiting, confirm, upload, share page, Gallery, Your places) are at least 44 by 44, as are the example cards' small switch buttons (drawn as a 32 px pill inside 44 px targets), Cancel on the status card in every placement, the notes' Next button and "Not your place?"; the footer's links keep the 24 px minimum. The quiet text links under the result picture (Share, Download, Gallery, and Replace photo, Change address and Delete in More), in the More and How does it look panels, in the gallery head and viewer, on Your places and on the share page are 44 px tall.
- `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` disables the reveal's sweep and the crossfade between variants, the indeterminate indicator's animation, the hero drift (the photograph holds still), the waiting and upload sheen and sweep, the light string's arrival and twinkle and the ripple after a poke, the status card's arrival and the pulse beside its label, the crossfade between notes, the ambient fade, the share page's arrival, the upload dock's rise and the gallery viewer's fade. The bulbs are lit and still, the pulse is three still dots, the notes change only when the person uses Next, and the upload step's progress hairline stays as a still line. Nothing else depends on animation. Under reduced motion nothing is delayed either: animation and transition delays are zero, so staged entrances (the landing blocks and the result's action row and rows) appear at once.
- Text resizes to 200% without loss of content; layouts reflow at 320 CSS pixels wide without horizontal scrolling.

### Hero crops

The layout uses two art-directed crops per hero, owned by the holidays spec: a portrait rendition on any viewport taller than wide (media query `(orientation: portrait)`, used by the server shell, its preload and the app) and the 3:2 landscape rendition otherwise, each covering the full-viewport hero frame around its focal point. The server shell's `sizes` is `100vw`, and the hero preload uses the same value. Covering the frame crops the photograph, and the crop keeps the recorded focal point and safe area in view. The holidays spec supplies the assets, sizes and rights records ([holidays-and-content.md#hero-and-example-art](holidays-and-content.md#hero-and-example-art)); the server-rendered shell selects the crop through `srcset` and `sizes` ([../architecture.md#landing-and-share-page-delivery](../architecture.md#landing-and-share-page-delivery)). Web performance targets for the landing page are owned by [../../PLAN.md#10-quality-bar-and-release-criteria](../PLAN.md#10-quality-bar-and-release-criteria) and measured in the field through the `web_vitals` event ([data-and-api.md#analytics-events](data-and-api.md#analytics-events)).
