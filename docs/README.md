# Hailiday

Hailiday is a web app that shows people their real home decorated for a holiday, from a US address through Google Street View or from a photo they upload, and then links them to decorations they can buy through affiliate programs. The US Release code exists and runs locally; no gate, pilot, Street View license or merchant approval exists yet ([Status](#status)). This repository also holds the plan it is built from: the product plan, the decision register, the system architecture, the specifications, the dated research and the executable backlog. This file is the entry point: what exists today, what the product owner must do next, where each kind of reader should start, and the conventions every document follows.

**Owns:** the owner action list; reading order by audience; documentation conventions (status labels, ID schemes, activation states and evidence labels, dating of external facts, the Verify marker, linking rules).
**Baseline:** planning baseline 2026-11-09; external facts retrieved 2026-11-08.

## What this is

The product promise is "See your real space beautifully decorated for a holiday, then find decorations that help you create the look" ([PLAN §1](PLAN.md#1-product-promise)). A visitor arrives at a seasonal hero for the holiday that is coming up, types an address or uploads a photo, and sees a believable decorated version of that exact place beside the original, with a Shop the look panel of related items below it. The thesis behind every choice: anyone can already decorate a photo with a general AI tool, so Hailiday wins only by being simple, beautiful and emotional, and the feeling of seeing one's own place transformed is what nudges people to decorate it for real ([PLAN §1](PLAN.md#1-product-promise), Fixed [D-49](decisions.md#d-49)). Everything in the plan traces back to the product owner's original request, [original_prompt.txt](original_prompt.txt), and that thesis: twelve requirements and eight Fixed decisions, mapped through every document ([PLAN §2](PLAN.md#2-requirements), [fixed decisions](decisions.md#fixed-decisions)). The product is named Hailiday as of 2026-11-12, by the owner's decision ([D-29](decisions.md#d-29)).

## Conventions

- **Audience split.** This file, PLAN and the decision register are written for the product owner and any newcomer. Architecture, the specifications and the backlog are written for engineers and AI coding agents to implement from without asking questions. Every file opens with its purpose, an **Owns** line and a **Baseline** line.
- **One home per fact.** Each rule, number and policy is stated in the file that owns it (the last column of the document map) and linked from everywhere else. When two documents disagree, the owner file listed in the document map wins and the other document is corrected.
- **Decision status labels.** Fixed (set by the product owner; only the owner changes it), Proposed (the recommended default; the build proceeds on it unless the owner amends it), Accepted (approved by the owner and recorded in the decision log), Open (needs a decision or evidence; a stated default applies until then). Documents cite a decision as, for example, "Proposed [D-12](decisions.md#d-12)" and never restate its record ([how decisions work](decisions.md#how-decisions-work)).
- **ID schemes.** Decisions `D-01` to `D-58`; user-stated requirements `REQ-01` to `REQ-12` and derived requirements `NFR-01` to `NFR-09` ([PLAN §2](PLAN.md#2-requirements)); backlog items as a prefix letter plus two digits, `B00` being repository hygiene ([backlog item prefixes](glossary.md#backlog-item-prefixes)); research claims `GM-nn`, `AI-nn`, `CM-nn` and `RC-nn` in the claims registers. IDs are never reused or renumbered.
- **Phases and gates.** Phases 0 to 5 are delivery phases with identical names in PLAN and the backlog; "Phase N" never means a product release, and the release is always called the US Release. Gates A to D are decision points whose full checklists live only in the [backlog](backlog.md#gates). The plan states dependencies, never dates, durations or staffing.
- **Activation states and evidence labels.** Every capability is `fixture`, `live_private` or `live_public` per country, recorded in `country_capabilities`. Completion is described as *engineering-complete* (works end to end in `fixture` mode with automated tests), *live-validated* (verified in `live_private` with recorded evidence) or *publicly activated* (`live_public`). Every evidence artifact is labeled `fixture`, `replay` or `live`, and fixture success never counts as live validation ([activation and completion](glossary.md#activation-and-completion)).
- **Numbers are configuration with defaults.** Limits, windows, sizes and thresholds are written as "default N (configuration)" in their owner file, and code reads them from configuration rather than constants.
- **External facts carry a date and a source.** Every statement about a vendor, API, price, law or contract term cites its primary URL with the retrieval date (2026-11-08 unless a row says otherwise) or links the research section that does; each claims register names a re-verification trigger per claim. Claude model facts come from Anthropic's model table cached 2026-10-06. Legal conclusions are marked "counsel to confirm"; every dollar figure is an illustration at list prices, not a quote.
- **Verify marker.** A genuine uncertainty is flagged once, in the file that owns the fact, as **Verify:** followed by the concrete check and its owner. Everywhere else the plan states its position plainly.
- **Vocabulary.** Terms, enum values, endpoint names, error codes, state names and event names come from the [glossary](glossary.md) and nowhere else; `code font` marks an exact identifier; US spelling throughout, including identifiers such as `canceled`. Retired synonyms appear only in the glossary's [retired terms](glossary.md#retired-terms) table.
- **Format and links.** GitHub-flavored Markdown with sentence-case headings, tables for enumerations, mermaid for diagrams and no emojis. Every planning document, including this file, PLAN and the original request, lives in `docs/`; the repository root is reserved for the monorepo the build creates ([repository layout](architecture.md#repository-layout)). Links between documents are relative and target a file's H2 headings (for example `specs/render-pipeline.md#lifecycle-state-machines` from this folder), an H3 or H4 heading where a narrower rule lives, or a decision's H3 (`decisions.md#d-15`); from a specification or research document, PLAN is `../PLAN.md`.
